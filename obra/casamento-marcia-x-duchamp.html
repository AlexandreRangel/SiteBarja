A work entitled Futurist – the marriage of Márcia X and Marcel Duchamp seems to hold out this possibility: a ghost/mannequin, wearing a dress (bridal gown?) of Marcia’s, has, where the head should be, a Marinetti (!!) fan from the 30’s. As a footnote/commentary, right there is (one of) the product(s) of the procession of ruptures in art history of the 20th century. Fusion and ephemeral inscription…the formula may be saved and re-applied. The angle is just right for poeticizing the world: reducing “a complex problem to simple, clear points…”. he monster’s dwelling The fantastic beast is always an offense to Creation, probably because it implies that something was left lacking the first time. Salah Stétié slyly insinuates that the appearance of exotic man-made bestiaries is nothing more than a symptom of the limits of God’s imagination (44). On the other hand, these fictitious animals also bear in their features evidence of all the anguish and terror projected onto them by humans, for whom they, like all other animals, whether wild or domestic, represent alien, impenetrable beings. Why not just say that this marvelous fauna, unholy marriage of dream and nightmare, is the symptom of the limits of man’s reality? Balanced on a line between God and Man, we begin to contemplate the question of objects, whether the inanimate is capable of distilling a live organism. The resulting hybrid body might be like the strange creature proffered by Wagner Barja in São João Beuys (1990) (St. John Beuys), in which a small household shrine dedicated, in this case, to St. John the Baptist, bizarrely metamorphoses into an animal appointed with a large lambskin tail. to be or not to be, for Gullar
